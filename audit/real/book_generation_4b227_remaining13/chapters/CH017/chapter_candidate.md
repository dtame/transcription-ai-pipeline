# Nothing by Chance

## Practice, Not Accident

Christianity is real, but it is not lived by chance. You do not drift into a life of faith; you practice your life deliberately, the way you would practice anything else you wanted to master.

Spiritual truths are only proven true through practice and use, not by mere declaration. We must practice them. You will know only when you use what you have been given, because using it is what proves that it works.

I practice it. I set my mind on it. This is not something that happens automatically; it requires daily, conscious attention to live in faith rather than in habit.

I live life, and I have an aura around me that will not allow certain things, because I am practicing life. An intentional practice of life surrounds a person with a kind of protection that prevents certain accidents from happening.

This intentionality extends even to speech. When I notice I have spoken wrong, I take it back. I cancel that one, and I say what I am supposed to say instead. Even when I am praying, if I say something that is not right, I correct it immediately: no, I cancel that one, this is what I want to say.

This same discipline of trained confidence shows itself in how one faces ordinary danger.

God told me that death has not been permitted to take me, and I have trained myself to walk in that confidence. When I enter a plane, I sleep. Training oneself spiritually in this way reflects a deep trust that death has no permission to take hold.

I was traveling with the Ethiopian airline once, and I was sleeping. The plane went through turbulence badly enough that one of the passengers stood up to pray out of fear. Afterward, some young people on the flight told me that when they saw me sleeping through it, they knew nothing bad would happen to that plane. They said, the man is sleeping, so don't worry yourself.

Christianity is real, but we must not judge it by people who are not practicing it. That is why Paul could speak of his own fatigue and labor and still say, 'Be my imitators as I imitate Christ' — he offered his own life, faithfully practiced, as the standard by which the faith should be judged.

## God Did Not Make Anyone Poor

I heard a pastor recently who was saying that God made poor people, drawing this claim from the book of Proverbs — that God himself is the maker of poor people.

God created everybody, but he did not make anybody poor. Whatever blessing God gives, he does not design poverty into anyone's life.

There are things in life that come down to choice. If you make a wrong choice, you will harvest badly. Some hardships people face in life are not imposed by God but are the harvest of choices that were wrong from the start.

To examine this more closely, look at how Proverbs itself speaks of the poor.

Proverbs chapter 19, verse 1 reads: 'Better is the poor man that walketh in his integrity.' A poor man who works with integrity is good — the emphasis of the text falls on integrity, not on poverty as something to be praised or sought for its own sake.

The same chapter also warns that a soul without knowledge is not good, and that whoever hastens with his feet sins. Lack of knowledge and hasty action lead to sin or failure.

It speaks too of a man who perverts his ways, his heart fretting against God — a picture of human folly. This can be framed in terms of a pattern inherited generationally, from ancestors, as opposed to a lineage found in God.

Other verses in the same passage speak of what wealth and poverty do socially.

Look at verse four: wealth makes many friends, as honey draws flies, while poverty drives them away. Multiple Bible translations render this verse the same way — wealth attracts, poverty repels.

God is the maker of us all, without exception. But the trajectory a person's life takes — toward poverty or toward wealth — is often shaped by choices, sometimes choices made before that person was even born, by their parents or ancestors. It may not be the individual's own direct choice, yet it still shapes the path.

I know Mama Jan. Nobody knew her; she was dragging her children through extreme poverty and facing the jealousy of others. But she made a choice: she said she would change the trajectory, and vowed that no child under her would ever again sleep without eating.

Some parents simply do nothing. They don't work, and then they find reasons to justify their condition — blaming it on something vague, or saying it's my ancestors, rather than taking responsibility themselves.

So do not go and say God made me like that. God did not make you like that, my friend. You have made a choice, or a choice was made for you before you were born — but it was not God who made you poor. Do not blame God for the consequences of choices that were never his.

## The A Part and the B Part

Our trouble is that we read the warning passages of Scripture without also reading the promises that stand beside them. There is what might be called the B part of a text — its warnings — and there is the A part — its promise of life. To live is Christ; whatever a person might otherwise be living under, you cannot live in Christ and remain under the curse that these warnings describe.

Deuteronomy 30:19 sets this choice plainly: I put that choice before you, choose life. The same structure appears already in Deuteronomy 13.

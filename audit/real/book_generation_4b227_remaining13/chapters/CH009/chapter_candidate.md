# Laws You Gave Yourself

## When Admiration Becomes Law

Spiritual experience is not general; it is always individual, always personal. What God does in one life does not become a universal standard that everyone else must now match.

I recognize how easily this truth gets lost. I look at my pastor, Mama Sabine, and I see her resilience, her consistency. I see the way she dances, the way she talks. An image forms in my head, and I desire that. But I know that I cannot be like that, exactly as she is. So, without realizing it, I begin to create laws in my head. She did not create the law. I create the law, and that law begins to constrain me.

But show me one condition God gave. Where do you get the law that you have to be like Bishop Chris in order to be known by God? God never laid down such a demand; it is self-imposed.

When you compare yourself to another person's spiritual attainment, wanting to 'be like' a famous minister or a figure you admire, there are things underneath that comparison. First, you despise yourself. Second, you think God doesn't know you. Third, you think you can impress God by becoming someone else.

## He Knows How He Made You

You are unique. You are the fruit of this unique person that you are, made deliberately and placed deliberately. He made you and dropped you exactly where you are. Do you think he is stupid? Do you think he made you and put you there so that a Japanese man is better than you?

He knows you as a person. He can create a law that would break you, but he cannot do it, because he knows you. He would be right just to do that, yet he does not, because of who you are to him.

He knows the village you came from. He knows that your parents, that the road, that the politicians, that even 2026, are all part of what he knows about you. So he disposes himself accordingly, and you can function like that, like Jesus functioned, despite your particular origin and hardship.

By the grace of God, I prayed. I fasted for years. God helped me, and I broke through. These testimonies of breakthrough are not instant achievements; they are the fruit of a long process.

This is not a hundred-meter dash. No. It's a marathon. You have to run this race all your life.

You are talking as though this God is one that, to reach him, you have to be this powerful, this extreme. Many believers overestimate the difficulty of drawing near to God, imagining him as a distant, demanding figure who requires extraordinary feats before he will receive them.

## The Real Cost of the Doctrine

In the time of Jesus, people lived under Roman military occupation. A Roman soldier could compel any passerby to carry his pack for one mile, simply by right of conquest. You are not who you think you are to resist such a demand; think of it like asking who Anguissa is in that position of forced compliance. And Jesus said, when a soldier tells you to go one mile, go two miles with him.

Think of it like meeting a colonial tutor who was also a colonel. He looks at your shirt and says, I like that shirt. For him to say that he likes it means you must give it to him. You do not ask questions when he says it. And Jesus taught that you should go and get the suit you bought the shirt with, and give him two of them as well.

These two illustrations, the soldier and the tutor, show what Jesus' teaching actually cost the people who first heard it.

Do not imagine that the doctrine of Jesus was an easy one. They slap you on one cheek, you give them the other. That was Jesus' doctrine, given under real, harsh occupation, not as a comfortable abstraction.

Sometimes when we are teaching, if we don't look at the Bible carefully, we reach for modern illustrations, democracy, freedom of speech, and when we think in those categories we behave in a certain way that can misrepresent the biblical reality underneath the text if Scripture itself is not checked.

Today, nobody forces you to come to church. You can identify with a congregation and then withdraw without needing to inform anyone. At most, your pastor might decide that your conduct is too much for the fellowship. This was not always so; church attendance and tithing were once enforced by civil and religious authority rather than left to voluntary participation.

There is a place in America called West Virginia where, by report, the pastor will call the police for you if you do not pay your tithe. Other American pastors have reported that if a man drives past the church on a Sunday, playing music, they call the police on him, saying, this man is a troublemaker.

Even in Switzerland, when they ask which church you are going to, a portion of your income, ten percent, is sent there directly. Even in Germany it is the same.

How did they kill Jesus? Religious authorities came disguised as those concerned with right doctrine, saying he does not agree with our religion, and political authorities colluded with them. Politicians said, we are going to tell the president, and Pilate simply washed his hands quickly. Historically, religious and political power collaborated to execute those who dissented, as happened with Jesus.

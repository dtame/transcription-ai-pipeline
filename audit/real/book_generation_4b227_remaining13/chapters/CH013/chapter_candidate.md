# The Renewed Mind and the Hidden Faculty

## Renewal Is Not Force

Scripture tells us to present our bodies as a living sacrifice, and this is an acceptable, agreeable service to God. That transformation happens through the renewing of our mind, as Romans chapter 12 verse 2 states.

Your brain is an instrument that God has given you for everything. It is like an engine, a motor. Used rightly, it can serve you powerfully; used wrongly, it produces a bad result.

Renewing the mind is not accomplished by force. It is not accomplished by the spirit alone. It requires something more deliberate than either of these: an intentional engagement of thought itself.

## The Power the Church Did Not Teach

Imagination is a faculty that God himself has given you. Even people who have never been to school, who have had no formal education, still have imagination. The problem is not that you lack imagination — the problem is that you have failed to use it or direct it rightly.

There is a kind of imagery that comes to mind without any effort at all. Picture the road between Douala and Yaoundé: the one image you see easily there is an accident, severed limbs, three feet cut off. Or picture going to see your husband, and the first image that comes is of him slapping you. Left undirected, the imagination fills itself with negative or trivial pictures rather than with godly ones.

There is also a hidden power at work here, one the church has, for the most part, failed to teach: this power is called the imagination.

Jesus himself was led by the Spirit into the desert, and there the devil tested him with a visualized temptation — to jump from the temple. Even in that testing, imagination under the Spirit's leading was present.

At the beginning, the earth was void and without form, and the Spirit of God hovered over it — open above it. This picture mirrors the potential of the human mind: it can be filled and made productive when the Spirit moves upon it.

Indians practice this. The Shintos, the Buddhists practice this. Transcendental meditation is taught by these traditions, and it is taught to businessmen and to national leaders. Yet those of us who are stewards of the mysteries of life have no knowledge of these things.

In the church, people say this is just positive thinking. It is not positive thinking. Faith is not positive thinking — faith is grounded in what we hope for.

## Bring Jesus Into the Thought

Even while praying in tongues, your imagination can wander off to unrelated, worldly concerns — like a dean who refused to give you good marks. In that state, your thought is left sterile, unproductive, even as your mouth continues to pray.

The remedy for this is to make that thought productive instead of leaving it sterile. Bring Jesus into your thought, and keep your thought fixed there.

We are instructed to let the mind that was in Christ be also in us. What is that mind that was in Christ? It is the connection that joins mind to the eternal spirit, so that fellowship can last.

The Father wants to influence your mind, not merely your body, because your spirit is already complete in him — but your mind is disjointed, separated from that connection, and he is working to join it back.

When the mind connects to the spirit in this way, your fellowship with God becomes eternal, and you can say things that the ordinary person cannot understand — what is supernatural to them becomes natural to you.

So when you kneel down to pray, imagine Jesus. Do not merely speak words absentmindedly — see him there before you.

Did you hear what Paul said? He told the Corinthians that he did not hinder them, even though he himself had been caught up into the third heaven, where things were said to him that he was told not to utter. This points to spiritual reserves far deeper than the ordinary requests we bring in prayer.

But if your mind is not there — if, while your mouth is praying, your thought is in China, or is occupied with malaria or with doctors — your spirit remains, so to speak, in prison. It cannot cross over into agreement with your soul.

And so we finish praying, and sometimes we say 'amen' aloud, while our spirit was never truly present in that prayer at all. Repeating the word outwardly does not guarantee that the mind was inwardly engaged.

## Training the Wandering Mind

If you are going to meditate, you must deal honestly with the fact that your mind will wander unless it is trained. Are you telling me that it will simply sit still on its own? It will not. You must arrest it, put it in a compartment, and say to it, 'I am talking to God now.' This discipline does not come automatically.

Train yourself in this way before sleep, and you will never have bad dreams again in the same way. More than that, you can learn to bring a dream back when it carries meaning worth recovering.

Samson's father, Manoa, gives us a picture of this. An angel suddenly appeared to his wife, but when she was afraid to speak, the angel went away. Manoa then prayed and asked that the man of God be sent back to them, and the angel returned. What this shows is that a divine encounter, once withdrawn, can be recalled through prayer — just as you can call a dream back.

When you are finished thinking about someone else's problem, ask yourself honestly whether your excessive worry has helped them at all. Often it has not — you have only added yourself into the problem. Your mother may be sick, but your anxious worry about it adds nothing to her healing and only increases your own burden.

Let us instead practice life the way a child does. A child's growth in any skill starts small; she must learn life step by step, and she must practice it. In the same way, spiritual life must be practiced gradually, growing from small beginnings.

Consider the difference between walking on dry land and being underwater. If you breathe the way you do on land while you are underwater, you suffocate; you have entered another dimension, and you must adapt consciously and correctly to it. Spiritual life works the same way — each dimension of truth requires its own proper adaptation, not the habits suited to another.

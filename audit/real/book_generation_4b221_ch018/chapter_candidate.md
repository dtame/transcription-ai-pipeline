# Testimonies of Resurrection

## The Word That Could Not Fail

I remember prophesying to a young lady that she would not die, that she would live. Afterward the lady died. God cannot lie, and I held to what had been spoken over her even when the natural outcome seemed to contradict it entirely.

They buried her, and they started digging the grave. Somehow, without any flyer, any hand bill, any radio announcement, a crowd gathered, and the Tribune newspaper was there as well. I don't know how news travels at times, but I noticed that nobody needed to be told. Only men who do abnormal things draw that kind of attention without any conventional means of spreading word.

At the graveside the devil said to me, look around, see this crowd — your life and your ministry are over today. But God had said to me that she would live, and I asked myself, can God keep to his word? I held to what had been spoken rather than to what the devil pointed at in that crowd.

I had learned how to pray for miracles: don't pray long prayers. Say a short prayer and stop. I was simply following the model of Jesus. So at that grave I prayed briefly, asking that her life return, and she came back looking fresh, and she spoke to me. It was later reported in the Tribune newspaper.

## Activation, Not Persuasion

Before I arrived, her body had already been prepared for burial. So I came not to produce something new out of nothing, but to release what God had already said. God said to Joshua concerning Jericho that as the people walked around the city, the wall would fall. Angels had already surrounded Jericho; they were just waiting for a sign, waiting for the shout, and then they pushed the wall down.

That same principle governs how we are to speak over sickness.

When you see a child who is sick at home, don't talk to the sickness. Don't talk to the demon. Learn to activate what God has already ordained by speaking directly to the person, the way Joshua's shout released what the angels were already prepared to do.

There is a real fear that attaches to this: if something doesn't happen as spoken, you fear you have killed their child, or taken someone away from the faith they knew before. But believing God's word, even carrying that fear of being wrong or of harming someone's trust, is central to operating in resurrection power. You can't help God. Believe him. Believe him.

## When It Does Not Happen

The boy died in church. He was dead in the church, with cotton in his nose and all of that. Rumors went around afterward that he had sold his soul to America. I said, go and bring him back. I said, Moussa, Jesus heals you now. He opened his eyes. He said, Brother Chris.

But it does not always happen that way. A woman, pregnant, eight months, had food poisoning, and she died at the hospital. I prayed in tongues. I prayed. Nothing happened. I told the husband, I said, she's dead. Go and bury her.

Afterward I asked, Father, why didn't you resurrect the girl? Not every prayer for healing or resurrection succeeds. The outcome belongs to God, not to the failure of the one who prays.

## The Vessel, Not the Name

Just be simple. When it doesn't happen, it's not your fault. You have done your part; let God do his part. The minister's role is to be an available, obedient vessel — nothing more is required, and nothing more should be claimed.

The most dangerous thing is performance — making healing or miracles about reputation. God has to do it. People will know about our church, but what's your business in that? It's the church of Jesus Christ, not a platform for a minister's name.

It's not your problem. Just be a vessel ready, a vessel ready to be used.
